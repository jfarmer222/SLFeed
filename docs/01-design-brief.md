# Design Brief — 65-SKU Stationary + Motion Upholstery Line

## The assignment

Build a 65-SKU upholstery line for a mid-market, ~125-store retailer running a
Good/Better/Best assortment strategy. Analytical and design-led: every SKU has
to earn its place on the floor aesthetically *and* survive a construction and
margin audit.

## What one SKU means here

One SKU is **one sellable piece** — a sofa, a loveseat, a chair, an ottoman, a
sectional configuration, a recliner. Covers are a separate axis: a family's
cover program multiplies against its pieces to produce orderable SKUs. The 65
pieces carry 598 orderable cover combinations, 499 of them stocked. See
`04-cover-strategy.md`.

## Design language: transitional, in three expressions

The whole line is transitional. That is a deliberate constraint — a 65-SKU
floor built from three unrelated style universes reads as a catalog dump, not
an assortment. Instead the line runs three *expressions* of one language, so
adjacent vignettes never fight each other:

| Territory | Arms | Posture | SKUs | Share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Classic Transitional** | Rolled → English | Warm, welted, nailhead at the top | 22 | 33.8% |
| **Modern Transitional** | Track → Scoop / Shelter | Lower backs, cleaner line, metal and brass | 22 | 33.8% |
| **Casual Transitional** | Slope → Pillow-top | Deep seats, loose backs, the sink-in family room | 21 | 32.3% |

A customer who likes the Ashcroft rolled arm and a customer who likes the
Harlow track arm are shopping the same floor without either feeling they've
walked into someone else's store.

## Tier architecture

19 frame families, 65 pieces:

| Tier | Families | SKUs | Share | Avg retail | Blended GM |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Good | 6 | 19 | 29.2% | $731 | 49.3% |
| Better | 8 | 29 | 44.6% | $1,198 | 52.3% |
| Best | 5 | 17 | 26.2% | $1,967 | 54.7% |

Better carries the most SKUs because that is where a healthy mid-market
assortment does its volume. Good exists to anchor the category and drive
traffic; Best exists to give the committed customer somewhere to go and to
carry the margin rate.

## The engineering thesis

Three decisions shape the whole line.

**1. Spend where the customer's body is, not where the spec sheet is.**
Two features were specced into the Best tier and then deliberately cut:
8-way hand-tied suspension and coil-on-coil seat construction. Hand-tying costs
roughly $195 of an $810 COGS budget on a sofa and, per the category's own
received wisdom, cannot be distinguished from well-executed pocketed coil in a
ten-minute showroom sit. That money went into pocketed coil suspension,
down-blend back pillows, mortise-and-tenon joinery and kiln-dried maple —
things the customer either feels immediately or genuinely benefits from over
fifteen years. See `02-feature-ladder.md`.

**2. Down goes in the backs, not the seats.**
A down-blend seat envelope runs ~$55 per seating position. Across a seven-seat
modular sectional that is $385 before labor, and it broke the Best sectional
band outright. Down-blend *back* pillows cost a third as much, and the
shoulders are where the customer actually registers the softness. Seats run
2.2 lb HR foam with a thick fiber wrap.

**3. Hold the frame constant, differentiate on what's visible.**
Lanmore is Harlow's silhouette in sectional form — same arm, same legs, same
cover program, same tooling. Kingsley is Ivywood's construction in modular
form. Ravenswood's sofa is built on the Ravenswood modular platform. Sharing
platforms across pieces is what lets 19 families cover 65 SKUs without 19
separate tooling investments.

## Trade-up lanes

A tier average proves nothing. What matters is whether an individual customer
can walk a like-for-like path from Good to Best. Every lane in the line:

| Lane | Good | Better | Best | Total spread |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Classic Transitional sofa | Ashcroft $849 | Glenwood $1,299 | Pemberton $1,799 | +112% |
| Modern Transitional sofa | Brantley $799 | Harlow $1,199 | Quincy $1,749 | +119% |
| Casual Transitional sofa | Cordell $899 | Ivywood $1,349 | Ravenswood $1,899 | +111% |
| Sectional (3-Pc) | Denby $1,399 | Kingsley $1,999 | Ravenswood $2,699 | +93% |
| Sectional (2-Pc chaise) | Denby $1,099 | Lanmore $1,599 | — by design | +45% |
| Recliner | Easton $499 | Marchetti $699 → Northfield $899 | Stratton $1,299 | +160% |
| Reclining sofa | Easton $999 | Marchetti $1,449 → Northfield $1,899 | Stratton $2,799 | +180% |

The three stationary sofa lanes land at 111–119%, inside the 80–125% guidance
for a three-rung ladder. Motion runs four rungs and a wider spread on purpose —
the manual-to-power transition is a genuine product discontinuity that deserves
its own step, so Better carries two rungs.

## Target customer by tier

**Good** — cross-shopping a warehouse club or a big-box floor. Deciding whether
this store is worth the trip. The Good product must not look like a
compromise; it must look like a deliberate design that happens to be
accessible.

**Better** — replacing a first sofa that went flat. Knows what they didn't like
about the last one. Will pay for a seat that holds up and a cover they don't
have to protect from their own family. This is where the volume lives.

**Best** — has decided to invest and wants to stop thinking about it. Responds
to construction they can verify by touch and to design specificity. Buys once.
