# Feature Ladder — What Changes Between Tiers, and Why

Features are not sprinkled upward. Each one is gated to the tier where its cost
is affordable, its benefit is perceptible, and its manufacturing complexity is
supportable. This document states the gate for every feature in the line.

## Stationary feature matrix

| Feature | Good | Better | Best | Why gated here |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Arm profile** | Rolled, track, slope | English, pillow-top track, welted track | Scoop, shelter, English + nailhead | Arm is the #1 style signal and the first thing touched. Complexity rises with tier; scoop and shelter need sculpted foam and precise tension, which is QC risk at volume prices. |
| **Back** | Tight / attached pillow | Loose pillow, channeled poly | Loose pillow, down-blend fill | Attached backs at Good buy the plush look with no fluffing complaints. Loose backs cost more per position and are the visible Better step-up. Down fill at Best is felt against the shoulders. |
| **Seat cushion** | 1.8 lb HD poly + dacron | 2.0 lb HR foam + dacron wrap | 2.2 lb HR foam + thick fiber wrap | The single highest-ROI upgrade in furniture — the customer feels it in the first five seconds and again in year three. Density climbs every tier without exception. |
| **Suspension** | Sinuous 8.5 ga, 4" spacing | Sinuous 9 ga, 3" spacing → pocketed coil | Pocketed coil drop-in | Invisible in the showroom, decisive at year five. Pocketed coil enters at Better on the comfort-led families (Ivywood, Kingsley) and is universal at Best. |
| **Frame** | Engineered hardwood, corner-blocked | Kiln-dried hardwood | Kiln-dried hardwood / maple | Engineered wood is dimensionally stable and correct at Good — solid hardwood is a credible Better talking point, not a Good one. |
| **Joinery** | Staple-and-glue + corner blocks | Dowelled + corner blocks | Mortise-and-tenon at stress points | Pure labor cost, entirely invisible, and the reason a Best frame carries a 15-year warranty. This is the tier's ethical obligation. |
| **Legs** | Resin, tapered wood | Turned wood, brushed metal | Hand-finished wood, solid brass | Cheapest visual upgrade available. A $25 leg swap changes the read of the whole piece. |
| **Fabric** | Poly-blend performance, 15–20k rubs | Performance chenille / textured, 35–50k | Premium woven, velvet, Crypton, top-grain leather, 60–100k | Performance is standard even at Good — the COGS delta has collapsed and it removes the "will it stain?" objection at the traffic price. |
| **Cover count** | 4–5 | 8–12 | 6–18 + custom-order book | See `04-cover-strategy.md`. |
| **Detail work** | Self welt | Contrast welt, channeling | Nailhead, scroll, hand-applied trim | High perceived craftsmanship per dollar of COGS. |

## Motion feature matrix

| Feature | Good (Easton) | Better (Marchetti / Northfield) | Best (Stratton) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mechanism** | Two-position push-back (chair); 3-position manual (sofa) | Wall-saver 3-position manual; rocker chassis → single-motor power | Dual-motor power + independent power headrest + power lumbar |
| **Demo moment** | The first recline | Push it against the wall, then recline it | Body back, head forward for the TV |
| **Frame** | Engineered + steel reinforcement plates | Kiln-dried + steel bolt-through at stress points | Kiln-dried + full steel bolt-through |
| **Seat** | 4.5" 1.8 lb HD poly | 5" 2.0 lb HR foam | 5" 2.2 lb HR over encased spring unit |
| **Power / tech** | — | USB-A port | USB-A + USB-C, battery backup |
| **Leather** | PU leather-look | Genuine split-grain (+$200) | Top-grain (+$300) |

**Why motion gets four rungs.** The manual-to-power jump is the largest single
product discontinuity in the line. Collapsing it into one Good→Better step
would either strand the manual customer or make power unaffordable at Better.
Marchetti (manual, wall-saver, $699–$799 chairs) and Northfield (single-motor
power, $899) are both Better, and the ladder is smoother for it.

**Why leather matters more here.** The association between recliners and
leather is deep enough that genuine leather has to reach Better in motion,
where in stationary it can wait for Best. Both Marchetti and Northfield carry
split-grain leather at a $200 upcharge.

## The two features that were cut

Good product engineering is as much about what you refuse to build.

### 8-way hand-tied suspension — specced, then cut

- **Cost:** ~$65 per seating position, ~$195 on a sofa — roughly 24% of
  Pemberton's entire $810 COGS budget.
- **Perceptibility:** effectively zero in a showroom sit against well-executed
  pocketed coil.
- **Benefit:** real, but purely longevity.
- **Verdict:** cut. Pocketed coil delivers most of the experience at under half
  the cost. The freed budget went to down-blend back pillows, kiln-dried maple,
  and mortise-and-tenon joinery — one felt immediately, two that genuinely
  extend the life of the frame.

Keeping it would have been textbook feature-dumping: loading the Best tier
until the retail price outruns willingness to pay.

### Coil-on-coil seat construction (Quincy) — specced, then cut

A pocketed coil cushion over a pocketed coil suspension is a great story and a
$45-per-seat cost. On Quincy it competed directly with the scoop arm and the
brass legs — the two things that actually sell that SKU. The arm is the
product; the budget follows the arm.

## Stacking priority

When a step-up budget is limited, this is the order to spend it:

1. **Seat cushion** — felt in five seconds, and again in year three.
2. **Cover depth and construction** — second-most-visible differentiator, and
   the cheapest perceived-choice-per-dollar in the line.
3. **Arm profile or arm detail** — if the arm changes, make it obvious; if it
   doesn't, add welt, channeling or nailhead.
4. **Suspension** — invisible now, decisive later. The right thing to do.
5. **Frame and joinery** — the Best tier's obligation to the customer.
6. **Legs and trim** — cheapest visual upgrade available; punches far above its
   COGS.

## Value multiplier check

For every $1 of COGS added between tiers, the customer should perceive $2–3 of
retail value. Applied to this line's actual step-ups:

| Step-up | COGS delta | Retail delta | Multiplier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ashcroft → Glenwood (Classic sofa) | +$187 | +$450 | 2.4× |
| Brantley → Harlow (Modern sofa) | +$165 | +$400 | 2.4× |
| Cordell → Ivywood (Casual sofa) | +$189 | +$450 | 2.4× |
| Glenwood → Pemberton | +$199 | +$500 | 2.5× |
| Harlow → Quincy | +$229 | +$550 | 2.4× |
| Ivywood → Ravenswood | +$221 | +$550 | 2.5× |
| Northfield → Stratton (power recliner) | +$149 | +$400 | 2.7× |

Every transition lands in the healthy 2–3× band. Nothing in the line is a
step-up the customer pays for and cannot perceive.
