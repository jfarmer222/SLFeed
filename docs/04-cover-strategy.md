# Cover Strategy & SKU Proliferation

Cover strategy is where design choice collides with inventory risk. Every
additional cover is more perceived choice on the floor and more markdown
exposure in the DC. This is the line's position on that trade.

## Cover depth by tier

| Tier | Covers per family | Pricing | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Good** | 4–5 | Single grade, no upcharge | No pricing complexity at the traffic-driving price point. The customer picks a color, not a budget. |
| **Better** | 8–12 | Two grades, +$100 premium | Choice opens up and a second grade creates trade-up inside the tier without adding SKU count. |
| **Best** | 6–18 | In-stock + custom-order book, +$150–$400 | The custom-order book carries breadth without carrying inventory. |

## Proliferation math

| Tier | Pieces | Stocked SKUs | Orderable SKUs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Good | 19 | 80 | 80 |
| Better | 29 | 274 | 274 |
| Best | 17 | 145 | 244 |
| **Total** | **65** | **499** | **598** |

**99 orderable SKUs carry no inventory** — they exist only in the Best-tier
custom-order books. That is the single most important number on this page: the
line offers 598 combinations while stocking 499, and the 99-SKU difference is
pure breadth with the inventory risk transferred to a customer who has agreed
to wait 6–10 weeks.

Best tier is 26% of pieces but only 29% of stocked SKUs, despite carrying the
deepest cover programs in the line. Custom-order is what makes that possible.

## Gating logic

Three things decide which fabric construction is allowed at which tier.

**1. Cost per yard against the tier's tolerance.** A sofa consumes ~16.5 yards.
At $20/yd, top-grain leather is $330 of COGS before a single other component —
which is precisely why Thorne prices at $2,299 rather than inside the
$1,399–$1,999 fabric-sofa band.

**2. Perception match.** Velvet on a $799 sofa reads as incongruent; the
customer expects it north of $1,299. Perception gates as hard as cost does.

**3. Operational complexity.** Leather cuts, sews and QCs differently from
fabric. That overhead gates it to tiers whose retail can absorb it.

## Three deliberate exceptions

**Oakhurst runs 12 covers at one price.** Every cover in the program is
performance, and none carries an upcharge. The family-with-kids-and-a-dog
customer never has to weigh the color they want against the one they can afford
to ruin. It is the widest single-grade program in the line and it is the
family's entire reason for existing.

**Thorne runs 6 covers — below the Best range.** Leather *is* the product here.
Six well-chosen hides beat sixteen mediocre ones, and every additional leather
SKU is a large inventory commitment in a material that cannot be reordered in
small lots. Registered as an intentional exception in the health check.

**Motion carries genuine leather at Better.** In stationary, leather can wait
for Best. In motion the recliner-leather association is strong enough that
Marchetti and Northfield both carry split-grain at a $200 upcharge — a
category-specific rule, not an inconsistency.

## Palette architecture

Neutrals carry the line; color is where the tiers separate.

- **Good** — greige, dove, charcoal, ivory, one blue. Safest possible palette;
  these have to sell without an RSA present.
- **Better** — the Good palette plus sage, rust, deeper blues, one or two
  textures. Chenille and linen-look enter here.
- **Best** — the full range: velvet, Crypton, premium wovens, top-grain and
  full-aniline leather. The custom-order books carry the trend colors, so
  trend risk never touches stocked inventory.
