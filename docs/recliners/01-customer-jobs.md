# Customer Jobs — What the Recliner Gallery Has To Answer

**Written before a single SKU was chosen.** That order matters. A 65-SKU gallery
built forward from vendor line sheets becomes a catalog dump with a price on
each tag. Built backward from the customer, it becomes a floor where every slot
is answering a question somebody actually walked in with.

**Source discipline:** the customer jobs below are inferred from the category's
own language in `30-categories/recliners.md` and `40-evidence/recliners.md`
(comfort trial at point of sale, health and lifestyle purchase driver, La-Z-Boy
as a generic term, lift growing 3x faster than base). They are labeled
**Proposal** under `CLAUDE.md` output labels — they are a merchant's reading of
the customer, not an approved policy record.

---

## The category's one unfair advantage

> *"Purchase driven by comfort, health, and lifestyle. La-Z-Boy brand is so
> dominant customers use it as a generic term for reclining chairs. Comfort
> trial is critical at point of sale."*
> — `30-categories/recliners.md`

Two facts, and they point the same direction:

1. **The customer has to sit in it.** This is one of the last categories where
   the store beats the internet outright. Nobody buys a $1,099 recliner from a
   phone without sitting in one first.
2. **The brand does the pre-selling.** The customer arrives already knowing the
   word "La-Z-Boy." That is 64% of the floor doing work before an RSA speaks.

The gallery exists to convert a sit into a step-up. Everything below is in
service of that.

---

## The nine jobs

Each job names the customer's actual sentence, what they are really buying, what
would make them walk, and how many of the 52 MDL slots it gets. Jobs overlap —
one chair can serve a second job — so each slot carries **one primary job** and
the counts below sum to 52.

---

### Job 1 — "I just need a recliner and I don't want to spend much"

**Buying:** permission to stop shopping. A functional chair, right now, at a
number that feels like a win.
**Walks if:** the $299 chair isn't there, or it's there and looks like a trap.
**Slots:** 3 MDL (Good) + the bulk of the 13 off-slot SKUs.

This is the traffic job. The merchandise matrix backs it with a **$299
doorbuster and a $399 promo price at 70% national-brand allocation** — this
customer is not buying "cheap," they are buying *a name brand at a price*. The
category's own marketing line is exact: *"Name brand at a price — Vail and
Collage $299."*

**The critical structural point:** the $299 chair sits **off-slot**. The Target
Slot Count definition excludes special buys and promotional items, which means
we can advertise $299 as loudly as we want without spending a strategic slot on
it. The promo does its job — get them in the gallery and into a seat — and the
governed 52 slots stay free to do the margin work.

---

### Job 2 — "This is the first real chair I've ever bought"

**Buying:** an upgrade from a hand-me-down, a Facebook Marketplace find, or
nothing. First household formation.
**Walks if:** the step from $299 to the next chair isn't visibly worth it.
**Slots:** 7 MDL (Better, lower).

This is the single most important conversion in the category and the one the
current mix under-serves. Good holds 11% of slots and returns **5% of sales on
8% of units** — it sells, but it sells *down*. The $399→$699 corridor is where
that either turns into margin or leaks back out the door.

The step-up here cannot be a spec-sheet argument. It has to be something the
customer feels in the ten seconds they are sitting down: a better seat, a
cover that doesn't feel like a rental, arms that hold their shape.

---

### Job 3 — "I want MY chair"

**Buying:** territory. This is the chair nobody else in the house sits in.
**Walks if:** it isn't comfortable in the first sit, or the power doesn't work
the way they expect.
**Slots:** 12 MDL (Better, core).

The volume engine. Better is **78% of slots, 76% of sales, 76% of units** — a
tier performing exactly at its slot share, which is what "Hold" means and why
the direction is Hold. This is where power recline enters and where La-Z-Boy's
name equity converts at full strength.

---

### Job 4 — "Will it even fit?"

**Buying:** relief. They have measured the room and they are not confident.
**Walks if:** every chair on the floor is 40" wide and needs a foot of wall
clearance.
**Slots:** 4 MDL (Better).

Apartment scale, wall-saver and wall-hugger geometry, tighter recline
footprints. This job is quietly one of the largest and it is almost never
merchandised as a job — the customer has to discover it by reading spec tags.
It should be signed on the floor.

---

### Job 5 — "It has to survive my life"

**Buying:** insurance. Kids, dogs, a toddler with a juice box.
**Walks if:** the cover looks precious.
**Slots:** 5 MDL (Better).

Performance fabric, cleanable covers, faux leather and leather-match for the
wipe-down story. This is a **cover-led** job, not a mechanism-led one, which
makes it the cheapest trade-up we own: the same frame in a better cover is a
visible, touchable upgrade at low incremental cost.

---

### Job 6 — "I don't want it to look like a recliner"

**Buying:** a chair they aren't embarrassed by. The living room is visible —
to guests, to a camera, to a roommate.
**Walks if:** the only options are overstuffed and brown.
**Slots:** 5 MDL primary (2 Good, 3 Better) — plus a secondary attribute across the floor.

**This is the Gen Z / Millennial answer, and it is an assortment job, not a
marketing one.** The under-35 buyer is not anti-recliner; they are anti-*that*
recliner. What converts them: push-back hi-leg and swivel-glider silhouettes
that read as accent chairs, tighter scale, boucle and textured performance
weaves, exposed wood or tapered legs, and USB-C rather than a legacy USB-A port
that signals the chair was designed a decade ago.

The category's own 2026 product plan already points here — *"adding storage,
wireless charging, cupholder to new cover"* — it just hasn't been aimed at this
customer on purpose.

**And the real barrier is not the chair.** The category's active content flag is
**Digital Commerce grade F overall, F on images, F on UPC, D+ on copy across 243
recliner products.** This customer researches before they visit. A chair with no
usable photography does not exist to them. See `04-genz-millennial.md` — the
assortment work is necessary but it is not sufficient.

---

### Job 7 — "I need one built for someone my size"

**Buying:** to be accommodated without having to ask.
**Walks if:** they sit down and the arms are tight or the chair feels flimsy.
**Slots:** 3 MDL (2 Better, 1 Best).

Big-and-tall scale, higher weight capacity, taller backs, extended footrests.
Structurally underserved across the market, which makes it a share opportunity
rather than a defensive slot. Requires no discount to sell.

---

### Job 8 — "Getting out of a chair is getting harder"

**Buying:** independence, and usually not for themselves — often a daughter or
a son is in the store too.
**Walks if:** the lift chairs are merchandised like medical equipment.
**Slots:** 9 MDL (lift, own ladder).

**The fastest-growing thing in the category: lift is +18.4% YoY against +4.5%
for base recliners — 3x the rate — on $4.47M of 6-month written business.**

This job gets its own parallel ladder, deliberately. Lift retails run high
enough that folding them into the recliner GBB bands would distort the mix and
make the Best tier look twice its real size (conflict C-013). Merchandised as
comfort and design first, mobility second — the customer who needs a lift chair
does not want a chair that announces it.

---

### Job 9 — "I want the chair that does everything"

**Buying:** proof that this store sells serious furniture, and a bit of
aspiration.
**Walks if:** the top of the floor is only $200 above the middle.
**Slots:** 4 MDL (Best).

Heat, massage, zero-gravity positioning, top-grain leather, memory positions.
**Best already turns 11% of slots into 19% of sales — a 1.7x sales-per-slot
premium, the best-performing real estate in the gallery.** It also does halo
work for every chair below it: the $2,999 chair is the reason the $899 chair
feels like a sensible decision.

## Slot allocation rollup

Each slot carries exactly **one primary job**, so the counts sum to 52 without
double-counting. Tier placement is constrained by the governed GBB targets —
Good 5 / Better 33 / Best 5 on the 43 recliner slots — which is why Job 1 gets
3 slots rather than 5: Good is only five slots wide, and Job 6 has a stronger
claim on two of them.

| Job | Primary customer | MDL slots | Good | Better | Best | Lift |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 · Cheap and now | Traffic / price-access | 3 | 3 | — | — | — |
| 2 · First real chair | New household | 7 | — | 7 | — | — |
| 3 · My chair | Core comfort buyer | 12 | — | 12 | — | — |
| 4 · Will it fit | Small-space | 4 | — | 4 | — | — |
| 5 · Survive my life | Family / pets | 5 | — | 5 | — | — |
| 6 · Not a recliner | Design-conscious under-35 | 5 | 2 | 3 | — | — |
| 7 · Built my size | Big and tall | 3 | — | 2 | 1 | — |
| 8 · Easier to stand | Aging in place | 9 | — | — | — | 9 |
| 9 · Does everything | Wellness / aspiration | 4 | — | — | 4 | — |
| | **Total MDL slots** | **52** | **5** | **33** | **5** | **9** |
| — | Doorbusters, special buys, quick-ship, color flex | 13 off-slot | — | — | — | — |
| | **Total floored SKUs** | **65** | | | | |

Recliner-only slots (Jobs 1–7, 9) = **43**, split Good 5 / Better 33 / Best 5 —
matching the governed 11 / 78 / 11 target exactly. Lift slots (Job 8) = **9** on
a parallel ladder. GBB is governed on the 43; lift is reported separately.

**On Job 6's five slots.** That looks thin for the job the whole brief hangs on,
and it would be, if five slots were the whole answer. They are not. "Doesn't look
like a recliner" is a *primary* job for five chairs and a *secondary* attribute
on many of the Job 2, 3 and 4 chairs — an apartment-scale wall-saver bought by a
young couple is serving Job 4 and Job 6 at the same time. Under-35 coverage is
therefore tracked as an attribute flag across the whole assortment, not by this
column. The validator enforces a floor on it independently.

## What this means for the ladder

Working backward from the jobs produces three rules the price ladder has to obey,
and they are not the same rule stated three ways:

1. **The $299 is a door, not a destination.** It is off-slot, heavily advertised,
   and its only job is to start a conversation and put someone in a seat. Judge
   it on traffic and on step-up rate, never on its own margin.

2. **The money is made between $499 and $1,099.** Jobs 2, 3, 5 and 6 all live
   here and together they hold 29 of 43 recliner slots. Every rung in this
   corridor needs one upgrade the customer can see from ten feet and feel in ten
   seconds. This is where "promote $299, margin out above it" either happens or
   doesn't.

3. **The top of the floor has to be genuinely top.** Job 9 is 4 slots doing
   disproportionate work — 11% of slots, 19% of sales — and it makes the middle
   of the ladder legible. Cut the ceiling and the middle stops reading as a
   value.
