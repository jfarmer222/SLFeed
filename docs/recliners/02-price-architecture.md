# Price Architecture — The Rails

The customer jobs in `01-customer-jobs.md` decide *what* the gallery has to
answer. This file decides *where those answers sit on a price line*. It defines
the rails only — which price points exist, how many slots each holds, and what
has to change between one rung and the next. Which vendor and model fills each
rung is settled in `03-trade-up-ladder.md`, after street pricing is verified.

Rails first, models second. Choosing models first is how a floor ends up with
four chairs at $899 and nothing between $499 and $799.

---

## The commercial argument in one table

From `30-categories/recliners.md`, current state:

| Tier | Slots | % Slots | % Sales | % Units | Sales per slot vs. fair share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Good — up to $399 | 5 | 11% | 5% | 8% | **0.45x** |
| Better — $400–$1,099 | 33 | 78% | 76% | 76% | **0.97x** |
| Best — $1,100+ | 5 | 11% | 19% | 16% | **1.73x** |

Read that bottom row again. **Best returns nearly four times the sales per slot
that Good does.** Good is not a bad tier — it is a *door*, and doors are not
supposed to be profitable. But it means the entire strategy reduces to one
sentence:

> Advertise the bottom of the ladder as loudly as the budget allows, and make
> sure nobody who walks in on a $299 ad stays at $299.

That only works if the step off $299 is visible, affordable, and immediate.

---

## Why the $299 does not get a slot

This is the structural move that makes "promote heavy, margin out above it"
actually work rather than just sound good.

The merchandise matrix defines Target Slot Count as:

> *"Total number of active MDL slots in a category. Sets the maximum SKU count
> for core assortment. **Excludes special buys, promotional, and in/out items**
> (per the promotional matrix)."*

So the $299 doorbuster, the LZB special purchases (Randell, Joshua), and the
quick-ship rapid-replenishment chairs are **floored but not slotted.** They are
13 of the 65 SKUs on the floor and 0 of the 52 governed slots.

The consequences are the whole point:

- We can advertise $299 at full volume — it costs us **no strategic slot**.
- The 52 governed slots stay free to carry margin architecture rather than
  promotional inventory.
- Good's MDL share stays at its 11% target while Good's *volume* grows through
  off-slot promo. That is how the category's "Grow" direction on Good is
  satisfied without taking slots from Better — recorded as conflict **C-012**,
  a Proposal, not policy.
- The promo is measured on **traffic and step-up rate**, not on its own margin.
  A $299 chair that converts at 30% to something above $599 is doing its job
  even at a thin margin. A $299 chair that sells 400 units and converts nothing
  is a loss dressed as a comp.

## The rails

### Good — 5 MDL slots, up to $399

| Price | MDL slots | Chairs | Role |
|---:|---:|---|---|
| $349 | 2 | Ashley Stayfish · Ashley Altari | The bridge. First step off the ad. |
| $399 | 3 | **La-Z-Boy Collage** · Ashley Kegler · Ashley Tulen | Good anchor — the matrix promo price point, and La-Z-Boy's verified floor. |

Two distinct price points, five slots. That is deliberate: Good does not need
price *choice*, it needs price *proof*. Three chairs at $399 in different covers
and silhouettes says "this is a real price, not a bait price" far better than
three chairs at three different numbers.

**La-Z-Boy Collage is the keystone.** Verified at **$399.99** — which is exactly
the promo price point the merchandise matrix specifies. The most recognized name
in reclining furniture, at the advertised number, in a governed slot.

**Off-slot underneath:** two $299 doorbusters and two $399 promo units.

The $299 → $349 step is $50 — the minimum perceivable move, and the most
important one in the category. It has to buy something the customer can see
from the seat: a better cover, a proper rocker mechanism, arms that hold shape.

### Better — 33 MDL slots, $400–$1,099

The volume engine. 14 distinct price points across 33 slots, averaging ~2.4
slots per rung, so each rung can show a silhouette choice or a cover choice
without fragmenting the ladder.

| Price | Slots | Reveal | Carried by |
|---:|---:|---|---|
| $449 | 2 | — cover and tailoring | La-Z-Boy Hawthorn · Franklin Kensington |
| $499 | 2 | **PUSH-BACK HI-LEG** — reads as a chair | **La-Z-Boy Charlotte** · La-Z-Boy Vail |
| $549 | 2 | **WALL-SAVER** — reclines from the wall | La-Z-Boy Rowan Wall · Finley Wall |
| $599 | 3 | **SWIVEL GLIDER** — the under-35 silhouette | **Franklin Kensington Swivel** · Ashley ModMax · LZB James |
| $649 | 3 | — swivel depth + cuddler scale | LZB Pinnacle Swivel · **Franklin Brutus Snuggler** · LZB Jasper |
| $699 | 4 | **POWER RECLINE** — the hinge | **Ashley Draycoll Power** · LZB Joshua · Reed · Morrison |
| $749 | 3 | — power depth | Franklin Yogi Power · LZB Trouper · LZB Astor |
| $799 | 3 | **POWER HEADREST** | **LZB Pinnacle Power Headrest** · Pinnacle · Scarlett Hi-Leg |
| $849 | 2 | — scale and space | LZB Pinnacle Wall · **LZB Randell** (big & tall) |
| $899 | 3 | **POWER LUMBAR** | **Ashley Rowlett** · LZB Fulton · LZB Kodie |
| $949 | 2 | — power + scale | Franklin Sultan Oversized · LZB Ava |
| $999 | 2 | **LEATHER** | **LZB Trouper Leather** · LZB Dorian |
| $1,049 | 1 | — leather depth | LZB Greyson Leather |
| $1,099 | 1 | Better ceiling | LZB Pinnacle Leather |

Every step is $50. That is not laziness, it is the point: **a $50 ladder with a
visible upgrade on every rung is the easiest thing in furniture to sell.** The
RSA never has to justify a big jump. They say "for fifty more dollars you get
the power," and the customer climbs one rung at a time until they stop.

**The hinge is $699.** Power recline entering below $700 is what makes the whole
ladder work — it is close enough to the $399 anchor that the customer can
imagine it, and far enough above that it carries real margin. Everything from
$449 to $649 exists to walk the customer to that chair.

### Best — 5 MDL slots, $1,100–$2,999

| Price | Slots | Reveal | Chair |
|---:|---:|---|---|
| $1,299 | 1 | **TECH** — storage arms, USB, cup holders | Franklin Denali |
| $1,599 | 1 | **LEATHER GRADE** — refined leather | Flexsteel Perfect Match Refined Swivel Power |
| $1,899 | 1 | — big-and-tall at Best | La-Z-Boy Roman Leather Oversize |
| $2,299 | 1 | **HEAT + MASSAGE** | La-Z-Boy Contour Leather SoCozi Power+ |
| $2,999 | 1 | **ZERO-GRAVITY** — the ceiling | Flexsteel Everest Power Swivel Glider |

Steps widen deliberately (+23%, +19%, +21%, +30%). Above $1,100 the customer is
no longer comparing chair to chair; they are deciding how much chair they want.
Narrow steps up here read as indecision.

**Tech enters at $1,299, not at $749.** That is a merchant decision, not an
accident — and an expensive one. La-Z-Boy holds 64% of the floor and has **no
verified charging, USB-C, cup-holder or storage-arm SKU at any price**, while
Franklin sells wireless charging at $768.99. Holding tech above $1,100 keeps the
volume corridor anchor-brand-led; the cost is that the under-35 answer below
$1,100 has to run on silhouette, scale and cover instead of ports. Logged as
conflict **C-017**.

**The $1,599 Flexsteel is the category file's own 2026 plan, verified.** The
architecture named "$1,299 fabric / $1,599 refined leather in BEST" as a 2026
product change. Both price points check out at Slumberland today.

**The $2,999 is not there to sell in volume.** It is there so the $899 chair
reads as sensible. Five slots doing 19% of category sales is the best real
estate in the building — cut the ceiling and the middle of the ladder loses its
reference point.

### Lift — 9 MDL slots, parallel ladder

| Price | Reveal | Chair |
|---:|---|---|
| $599 | Lift entry — 2-position | La-Z-Boy Jean |
| $749 | — value depth | Flexsteel Granite |
| $899 | **3-POSITION** | La-Z-Boy Miller (300 lb) |
| $1,099 | **POWER RECLINE** added | La-Z-Boy James Lift |
| $1,299 | — comfort depth | La-Z-Boy Emerald |
| $1,499 | **INFINITE POSITION** | Flexsteel Platinum |
| $1,799 | **HEAT + 6-MOTOR MASSAGE** | La-Z-Boy Clayton |
| $2,199 | **HEAVY DUTY** | Franklin Polaris |
| $2,799 | **SLEEP POSITIONING** — the lift ceiling | Flexsteel Zecliner 3+ Petite |

**Lift runs its own ladder and is never folded into the recliner GBB mix.**
Lift retails skew high enough that blending them would make Best look roughly
twice its real size and would misstate the mix the category is governed on.
This is conflict **C-013**, resolved structurally: two columns, never one
average. The blended view is reported for reference and governed on nothing.

Nine slots is a real commitment, and the evidence supports it — **lift is
+18.4% YoY on $4.47M of six-month written business against +4.5% for base
recliners.** Three times the growth rate of the category it sits inside.

## Ladder shape

Distribution of all 65 floored SKUs by $250 increment. No gap wider than one
increment anywhere between $299 and $1,099 — the corridor where 35 of 43
recliner slots and essentially all the volume live.

```
  $250–$499   ██████████████     14   Good MDL + off-slot promo + Better entry
  $500–$749   █████████████████████  21   the walk-up: hi-leg, wall-saver, swivel, POWER
  $750–$999   ████████████████   16   the engine: headrest, lumbar, scale, leather
$1,000–$1,249 ████                4   Better ceiling + lift power
$1,250–$1,499 ███                 3   Best entry (tech) + lift
$1,500–$1,749 █                   1   leather grade
$1,750–$1,999 ██                  2   big-and-tall Best + lift wellness
$2,000–$2,249 █                   1   heavy-duty lift
$2,250–$2,499 █                   1   heat + massage
$2,500–$2,749 ·                   0   deliberately empty — see below
$2,750–$2,999 ██                  2   the two ceilings — recliner and lift
```

**51 of 65 SKUs — 78% — sit between $250 and $999.** That is what a value
retailer's recliner gallery is supposed to look like: dense where the customer
actually transacts, thinning smoothly upward, with a visible top rather than a
cliff.

**The one empty increment is above the volume corridor and is deliberate.**
Nothing sits between $2,500 and $2,749. At that altitude the customer is no
longer stepping, they are choosing a destination, and a chair there would only
blur the $2,299 massage rung and the $2,799/$2,999 ceilings. The validator only
enforces continuity between $299 and $1,099 for exactly this reason.

---

## The three rules this ladder has to keep

1. **Fifty dollars, one upgrade.** Through the entire $349–$1,099 corridor, every
   step is $50 and buys exactly one new thing the customer can see or feel. Two
   upgrades on one rung is a wasted reason to trade up — the next rung now has
   nothing to offer.

2. **Power at $699.** The single most important number on the floor after $299.
   If power recline drifts to $899, the ladder breaks in half and the $399
   customer has nowhere to climb to.

3. **The ceiling stays visible.** $2,999 at the top of the recliner ladder and
   $2,999 at the top of lift. The halo is what makes the middle legible.

---

## Validation

These rails are enforced by `scripts/recliner_health.py`:

- no two distinct price points closer than $50
- no step above 45% below $1,100, or 60% above it
- no empty $250 increment between $299 and $1,099
- Good 5 / Better 33 / Best 5 on the 43 governed recliner slots (±1)
- no SKU above $2,999
- the $299 exists, and **fails the build if it ever lands on an MDL slot**
