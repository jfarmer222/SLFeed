# The Gen Z / Millennial Question — Answered Honestly

The brief asked: *somewhere address the question, what about Gen Z and
Millennials?*

Here is the answer, and the first half of it is uncomfortable.

---

## BLUF

**The under-35 customer is not rejecting recliners. They are rejecting the
recliner they picture when you say the word — and they are rejecting it on a
phone, before they ever walk into the gallery.**

The assortment fix is real but small: 14 of 65 SKUs now carry under-35
attributes, 10 of them at or below $999, built on **silhouette, scale and cover**
rather than technology. The bigger fix is not an assortment problem at all:

> **Digital Commerce grade F overall. F on images. F on UPC. D+ on copy.
> Across 243 recliner products.**
> — `30-categories/recliners.md`, active flags

A chair with no usable photography does not exist to a 27-year-old. We can
curate the most intelligent recliner floor in the Midwest and they will never
see it, because the shopping journey that ends in our gallery **begins on a
screen we are failing at.**

---

## What they actually object to

Not the function. Nobody dislikes putting their feet up. Three specific things:

1. **The silhouette.** Overstuffed, wing-backed, visibly a recliner from across
   the room. The object announces itself.
2. **The scale.** Most recliners are built for a family room in a house. They
   are buying for an apartment, a rental, a first condo.
3. **The signal.** The living room is visible — to guests, to a roommate, to a
   camera. A recliner reads as a piece of furniture from someone else's life
   stage.

Price is *not* the objection. They will pay $699. They will not put *that chair*
in *that room*.

The merchant decision on this build holds tech reveals above $1,100 (conflict
**C-017**), because La-Z-Boy — 64% of the floor — has no verified charging SKU
at any price. So the under-35 answer below $1,100 runs on the three levers that
don't need a power supply.

in two scales with scale-led signage.** It costs no additional styles.

## The real barrier: the content flag is the Gen Z strategy

This is the part that matters more than the chairs.

| Digital Commerce grade | Score |
|---|---|
| Overall | **F** |
| Images | **F** |
| UPC | **F** |
| Features | C+ |
| Copy | **D+** |
| Products affected | **243** |

The under-35 buyer researches first and visits second. Our own category file
says *comfort trial is critical at point of sale* — which is true, and it is
exactly why the content failure is fatal rather than merely unfortunate. **The
online listing's only job is to get them into the seat.** Right now it can't do
that job, because there is no image.

## What this build does not fix

Say it out loud rather than let it be discovered later:

- **Tech is above $1,100.** Charging, USB-C and cup holders are exactly what
  this customer expects as table stakes, and our decision puts them out of reach
  in the band where under-35 buyers transact. The chair that has them — Franklin
  Magnus at $749 with built-in USB — sits **off-slot**. That is a deliberate,
  reversible choice, not an oversight. It should be revisited the moment
  La-Z-Boy will build a charging SKU. (**C-017**)
- **No boucle.** Needs a line sheet.
- **In-stock is 85% against a 95% goal.** A customer who researched a specific
  chair and drives to the store to sit in it, and it isn't there, does not come
  back. The under-35 strategy depends on the operational gate closing.

---

## Measuring it

Do not measure this on "Gen Z sales," which nobody can attribute cleanly.
Measure it on:

1. **Step-up rate off the $299/$399 ad** — the whole margin thesis in one number.
2. **Sell-through on the 14 flagged SKUs vs. the floor average.**
3. **Digital Commerce grade on the 14 remediated SKUs** — F to B is the target,
   and it is the only one of these we fully control.
4. **Wall-saver and swivel-glider unit share** — if the silhouette answer is
   right, these outrun their slot share.
