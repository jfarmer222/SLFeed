## Why it never merges into the recliner GBB

Lift retails skew high. Six of the nine lift slots sit at $1,299 or above, which
in recliner terms is Best. Fold them in and the Best tier reads as **10 of 52**
instead of **5 of 43** — roughly double its real size — and the mix the category
is governed on becomes fiction.

So the build reports two columns and averages neither:

| Basis | Good | Better | Best |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **Recliner only — GOVERNED** | 5 | 33 | 5 |
| Blended incl. lift — reported only | 5 | 37 | 10 |

This is conflict **C-013**, resolved structurally. `40-evidence/recliners.md`
states the GBB basis itself: *"GBB Basis: based on recliner price."* The
governed row is the one that matches the source.

## Merchandising rule: comfort first, mobility second

The customer who needs a lift chair does not want a chair that announces it —
and very often they are not the one making the decision alone. An adult child is
standing next to them.

- **Never sign it "medical."** Sign it *easier to get up*, *power assist*,
  *stand with confidence*.
- **Merchandise it inside the gallery, not in a corner.** A lift chair isolated
  at the back of the department tells the customer what we think of them.
- **The top of the ladder is a design object.** The Zecliner is not a mobility
  aid with a price tag; it is a sleep-and-wellness chair that happens to lift.
- **Sit the adult child down too.** They are validating the purchase and they
  will not validate something that looks like hospital furniture.

## The Zecliner is the one genuinely defensible halo on the floor

Flexsteel positions it inside a broader **"Flexsteel Wellness"** platform, and it
carries something no other chair in this assortment has: **third-party clinical
measurement.** SleepScore Labs studied roughly 1,700 nights and reported
measurable improvement in sleep metrics and perceived pressure relief.

Two things follow:

1. **It justifies $2,799 without a discount.** Every other halo chair on this
   floor is justified by materials and mechanism. This one is justified by an
   outcome.
2. **The "3+ Petite" is a small-scale frame.** Aging in place, in an apartment,
   is a real and growing customer and almost nobody merchandises to them. Petite
   scale at the top of the ladder is a quiet advantage.

## Open items

- **"Flexsteel Perfect Match lift" is NOT VERIFIED.** The Perfect Match program
  announcement describes 5 models / 18 SKUs with no lift mention, and
  `flexsteel.com/collections/lift-recliners` returns an empty collection. The
  Flexsteel-branded lift chairs Slumberland sells — Granite, Ruby, Mica,
  Platinum, Stewart, Shaw, Zecliner — are **not tied to that program by any
  source found.** The category file's claim that Flexsteel is "adding non-lift
  via Perfect Match" is consistent with this: Perfect Match may be the *non-lift*
  program. Confirm with the vendor before writing it into a program brief.

- **Capacity has no dedicated slot.** Franklin fields an **Independence lift at
  $1,416.99 rated to 500 lb**, and Mega Motion (High Point: Furniture Plaza 100,
  Floor 1) builds FDA Class II lifts fitting 4'11"–6'6" at 325–500 lb. The
  heavy-duty customer is currently served only at $2,199 via Polaris. If the
  bariatric and big-and-tall lift customer is real in our markets, that is the
  tenth lift slot — and it should come out of Better recliners, not out of lift.

- **Lift specialists are not in the governed vendor set.** UltraComfort (IHFC
  C1165, Commerce Floor 11 — lifetime warranty on lift frame and mechanism) and
  Mega Motion are credible resources for the 30% other-brand allocation. Neither
  is proposed here, because the governed four cover all nine slots. Worth a walk
  at the October Market.
