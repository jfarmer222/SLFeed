## Verbatim quotes
> "In fiscal 2026, we purchased over 90% of this imported product from Vietnam, with the vast majority coming from five major suppliers. The prices we paid for imported products, including associated transportation, increased during 2026 compared with fiscal 2025, primarily due to the implementation of tariffs in the first half of the fiscal year along with higher ocean freight costs in the second half of the fiscal year resulting from rising fuel prices."

> "In fiscal 2026, approximately 90% of the upholstered units sold in North America were produced in the United States. This has been our supply chain design throughout history to support our businesses by offering customized products to consumers in a short time period. These supply chain operations allow us to mitigate the impact of tariffs on imported goods. While we remain subject to certain tariffs on most products directly imported into the United States, the exposure to our cost of goods in fiscal 2026 was minimal overall."

> "In February 2026, the U.S. Supreme Court issued a ruling invalidating certain tariffs previously imposed under the International Emergency Economics Powers Act ("IEEPA"). As a result of this ruling, we are eligible for a refund of tariffs previously paid on imported goods. The ultimate availability, timing, and amount of potential refunds of such tariffs remains uncertain and could be subject to further legal, regulatory, and administrative developments or actions."

> "In fiscal 2027, we anticipate potential price increases across various commodities due to economic and geopolitical volatility, tariff and trade policies, and general inflation related to our core materials."

## Relevance
Primary-source confirmation that La-Z-Boy (owner of England Furniture) has low direct tariff exposure because ~90% of North American upholstered units are US-made; imported casegoods (mostly Vietnam-sourced, not upholstery) carry the tariff risk. Confirms the Feb 2026 Supreme Court IEEPA ruling opened a path to tariff refunds. No plant closures or adverse operational news disclosed for La-Z-Boy/England in this filing — supports "no adverse news" for the core upholstery business, with tariff cost pass-through flagged as a forward risk.
