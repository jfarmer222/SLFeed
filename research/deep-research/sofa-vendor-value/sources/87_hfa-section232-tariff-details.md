# Advocacy Update: New Details on Section 232 Furniture Tariffs

- **Publisher:** Home Furnishings Association (HFA) — industry trade association, CEO Peter Theran
- **Date:** originally published 2025-10-03, updated 2025-10-03
- **Type:** industry
- **Accessed:** 2026-09-26 (fetched)
- **Credibility:** 4 — trade association official advocacy update summarizing the Presidential Proclamation and HTS-code analysis for its retailer members; directly actionable for the retailer's own sourcing
- **Bias:** medium — HFA represents furniture retailers' interests and frames the tariffs as disruptive, but factual tariff-rate claims are consistent with legal-alert sources (Wiley, Thompson Hine)
- **Stream:** E

## Verbatim quotes
> "Beginning at midnight on October 14, 2025, imports of soft wood, kitchen cabinets, bathroom vanities, and upholstered furniture will be subject to Section 232 tariffs. Importantly, there is no 'on the water' exemption."

> "Those effective rates, as of October 14, 2025, are: Soft wood – 10%; Kitchen Cabinets – 25%; increase to 50% beginning January 1, 2026; Bathroom Vanities – 25%, increase to 50% beginning January 1, 2026; Upholstered furniture (sofas, chairs, sectionals) – 25%; increase to 30% beginning January 1, 2026."

> "These Section 232 tariffs will apply to all countries EXCEPT for the UK, the European Union, and Japan. These nations have specific language in their trade agreements that caps these Section 232 tariffs at 10%, 15%, and 15%, respectively."

> "these Section 232 tariffs WILL NOT stack on top of reciprocal tariffs or International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA) duties affecting Brazil (40%), India (25%), Canada (10%), and Mexico (10%). However, these new Section 232 tariffs WILL stack on top of China's existing 20% IEEPA tariff and Section 301 duties of 7.5% to 25%."

> "Following an analysis of those HTS codes, these 232 tariffs focus on upholstered furniture seating, and the exposure to case goods/wood furniture is limited."

## Relevance
Core primary/industry source for the tariff picture: confirms the 25% Section 232 tariff on imported upholstered furniture (sofas, chairs, sectionals) effective Oct. 14, 2025, with a planned but later delayed increase to 30%, no on-the-water exemption, and stacking rules that make China-sourced product the most exposed. Directly frames why domestically-built (USA) upholstery from vendors like La-Z-Boy/England, Flexsteel, Hughes, Albany, H.M. Richards etc. has a structural cost advantage over import-heavy competitors.
